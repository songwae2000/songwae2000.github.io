Data Observatory pilot, UN Group of Independent Eminent Experts on the DDPA

2026 → Now

The pilot aims to monitor racism, racial discrimination and xenophobia worldwide without relying on governments to report on themselves. I lead engineering on the team designing the pipeline that would do it from public sources alone.

  • Counting reports would mostly measure press freedom and connectivity. So the design estimates what goes unreported, using capture-recapture across independent sources and a Bayesian hierarchical model in Stan, and would publish intervals, not raw counts.
  • Running an LLM on every document would cost too much at global scale, so we staged it. A cheap lexicon-and-embedding filter goes first, then a fine-tuned XLM-R classifier, and only what survives reaches the LLM.
  • Constrained decoding keeps extraction to a fixed JSON schema, so the output always parses. The model is allowed to say it doesn't know, and nothing about a country would publish until a person approves it.
  • Every published number would link back to the events, sources and archived snapshots behind it, so anyone can check a claim for themselves.

Publications

N-grams vs. Neural Models for Low-Resource African Languages: When to Switch?

Nana Sam Yeboah, Cajetan Songwae, Dennis Asamoah Owusu

2026

AcceptedNeurIPS 2026 Workshop: Africa in AI

We compared N-gram models with fine-tuned XLM-R, AfroXLMR and small from-scratch Transformers on MasakhaNEWS, AfriSenti and SIB-200, across up to ten languages from four language families. Whether neural models win depends on how well a language is covered in pretraining, not its language family. A Platt-scaled hybrid of N-gram and neural predictions improves on both for most languages, at no extra training cost.
